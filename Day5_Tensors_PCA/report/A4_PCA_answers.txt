Assignment 4: PCA MCQs - Answers
================================

Q1. What is the primary purpose of PCA?
Answer: B. Reduce dimensionality while preserving as much variance as possible
Explanation: PCA finds orthogonal directions (principal components) that capture maximum variance in the data, allowing dimensionality reduction with minimal information loss.

Q2. Suppose a dataset has 100 features. What can PCA potentially do?
Answer: B. Reduce it to a smaller number of principal components
Explanation: PCA can reduce 100 features to k < 100 principal components that capture most of the variance.

Q3. Why might we want to reduce the number of features?
Answer: D. All of the above
Explanation: Dimensionality reduction makes models computationally cheaper, enables visualization of high-dimensional data, and reduces redundancy/correlation among features.

Q4. PCA looks for directions in the data along which:
Answer: B. The variance is maximum
Explanation: Principal components are directions of maximum variance in the data.

Q5. Imagine points forming a long, thin cloud. Which direction is most likely to become the first principal component?
Answer: A. The direction along the long axis of the cloud
Explanation: The long axis has the most spread/variance, so PC1 aligns with it.

Q6. Why does PCA prefer the direction with maximum variance?
Answer: A. It captures the direction containing the most information about the data's spread
Explanation: Maximum variance directions capture the most information about data structure.

Q7. Before applying standard PCA, why are the features usually centered?
Answer: A. To make the mean of each feature approximately zero
Explanation: Centering (subtracting the mean) ensures PCA finds directions of variance around the origin, not around the mean.

Q8. Consider the data: [10, 12, 14, 16, 18]. After mean-centering, what happens?
Answer: B. The values are shifted so that their mean becomes 0
Explanation: Mean = 14. Centered: [-4, -2, 0, 2, 4]. Mean is now 0.

Q9. If PCA is applied without appropriate scaling to features measured in very different units, what can happen?
Answer: A. A high-scale feature may dominate the principal components
Explanation: Features with larger scales (e.g., meters vs millimeters) will have larger variance and dominate PCs. Standardization (z-score) is needed.

Q10. Suppose PC1 explains 70% of the variance and PC2 explains 20%. How much variance is explained by the first two components?
Answer: C. 90%
Explanation: Cumulative variance = 70% + 20% = 90%.

Q11. If the first three principal components explain: PC1 = 60%, PC2 = 25%, PC3 = 10%. What percentage of the original variance is represented by these three components?
Answer: D. 95%
Explanation: 60% + 25% + 10% = 95%.

Q12. Suppose a dataset has 10 features and PCA produces 10 principal components. Which statement is correct?
Answer: B. PCA has not necessarily reduced dimensionality
Explanation: PCA produces as many components as features. Dimensionality reduction only occurs if you keep fewer than all components.

Q13. Which statement about principal components is TRUE?
Answer: B. They are linear combinations of the original features
Explanation: Each PC = w₁×feature₁ + w₂×feature₂ + ... (weighted sum of original features).

Q14. Suppose: PC1 = 0.7×X₁ + 0.7×X₂. What does this mean?
Answer: A. PC1 is a linear combination of X₁ and X₂
Explanation: PC1 is a weighted sum (linear combination) of the original features.

Q15. Why are principal components usually uncorrelated with one another?
Answer: A. PCA constructs orthogonal directions
Explanation: PCs are eigenvectors of the covariance matrix, which are orthogonal (uncorrelated).

Q16. In the covariance-matrix interpretation of PCA, eigenvectors represent:
Answer: A. The principal directions
Explanation: Eigenvectors of the covariance matrix give the directions of principal components.

Q17. In PCA, eigenvalues are primarily associated with:
Answer: A. The variance captured by the corresponding principal components
Explanation: Eigenvalue λᵢ = variance explained by PCᵢ.

Q18. Suppose the eigenvalues are: λ₁=5, λ₂=3, λ₃=1, λ₄=0.5. Which principal component captures the most variance?
Answer: A. PC1
Explanation: Largest eigenvalue corresponds to PC1 (most variance).

Q20. Suppose your original dataset has 50 features, but the first 5 principal components explain 95% of the variance. What is a reasonable interpretation?
Answer: B. The data can potentially be represented using 5 dimensions while retaining about 95% of its variance
Explanation: This is the core idea of PCA - low-dimensional representation preserving most variance.

Q21. If you keep only the first 2 principal components, what are you doing?
Answer: B. Creating a 2-dimensional representation using the first two PCA directions
Explanation: You project data onto the subspace spanned by PC1 and PC2.

Q22. Why is PCA frequently used before visualizing high-dimensional data?
Answer: A. PCA can project data into 2 or 3 dimensions
Explanation: Humans can only visualize 2D/3D. PCA reduces dimensions for visualization.

Q23. You have a dataset with 100 dimensions. You reduce it to PC1 and PC2 and plot the points. What are you primarily trying to see?
Answer: B. Patterns, clusters, separation, and structure in a lower-dimensional representation
Explanation: Visualization reveals data structure (clusters, outliers, separation) in 2D.

Q24. Which statement is FALSE about PCA?
Answer: D. PCA requires class labels
Explanation: PCA is unsupervised - it does NOT use class labels. It only uses feature variance.

Q25. PCA is called an unsupervised dimensionality-reduction technique because:
Answer: A. It does not require target/class labels
Explanation: Unsupervised = no labels needed. PCA uses only feature covariance structure.

Q26. Does PCA guarantee that a machine-learning model will achieve higher classification accuracy?
Answer: B. No
Explanation: PCA maximizes variance, not class separation. It can discard discriminative low-variance directions.

Q27. Imagine two classes overlap heavily along the direction of maximum variance but are well separated along a low-variance direction. What could happen if PCA keeps only PC1?
Answer: A. Classification information may be lost
Explanation: PC1 captures max variance, not max class separation. Low-variance discriminative directions may be discarded.

Q28. PCA primarily optimizes for:
Answer: B. Maximum variance captured
Explanation: PCA finds directions of maximum variance, not maximum classification accuracy.

Q29. Which situation is most suitable for PCA?
Answer: A. A dataset containing many correlated numerical features
Explanation: PCA works best when features are correlated (redundancy can be removed).

Q30. Suppose you have two highly correlated features. What is PCA likely to discover?
Answer: B. One direction capturing most of the shared variation and another with relatively small variance
Explanation: Correlated features share variance. PC1 captures shared variation, PC2 captures residual.

Q31. If PC1 explains 98% of the variance, what might this suggest?
Answer: A. The data may have an approximately low-dimensional structure
Explanation: Very high variance in PC1 suggests data lies near a 1D subspace.

Q32. Which sequence best represents the conceptual workflow of PCA?
Answer: B. Center/scale → find directions of variance → rank components → project data
Explanation: Standard PCA pipeline: preprocess → eigendecomposition → sort by eigenvalue → project.

Q33. Imagine you have 1000-dimensional data. PCA reduces it to 20 dimensions while retaining 95% of the variance. Which statement is BEST?
Answer: C. The 20-dimensional representation captures approximately 95% of the variance present in the original data
Explanation: This correctly describes what variance retention means in PCA.
