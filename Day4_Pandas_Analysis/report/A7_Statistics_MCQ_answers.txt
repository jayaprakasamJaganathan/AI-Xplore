PART A — BASIC STATISTICS

1. What is the primary purpose of statistics?
A. To store data
B. To collect, analyze, interpret, and communicate data
C. To increase the size of a dataset
D. To convert all data into numbers
Answer: B. To collect, analyze, interpret, and communicate data

2. Which of the following is an example of quantitative data?
A. Eye color
B. Blood group
C. Height
D. Country
Answer: C. Height
Explanation: Quantitative data is numerical (measurable). Height is numerical.

3. Which of the following is an example of categorical data?
A. Age
B. Weight
C. Temperature
D. Blood group
Answer: D. Blood group
Explanation: Categorical data represents categories/groups. Blood group is categorical.

4. A population refers to:
A. A small subset of observations
B. The entire group being studied
C. Only the observations used for training
D. Only numerical observations
Answer: B. The entire group being studied

5. A sample is:
A. The entire population
B. A subset of the population
C. Always larger than the population
D. A single variable
Answer: B. A subset of the population

6. Why is sampling commonly used?
A. Studying the entire population may be expensive or impractical
B. Samples always provide perfect results
C. Populations cannot be measured
D. Sampling eliminates all errors
Answer: A. Studying the entire population may be expensive or impractical

PART B — MEASURES OF CENTRAL TENDENCY

7. Which measure represents the arithmetic average of a dataset?
A. Median
B. Mode
C. Mean
D. Range
Answer: C. Mean

8. What is the mean of: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10?
A. 5
B. 6
C. 7
D. 8
Answer: B. 6
Explanation: (2+4+6+8+10)/5 = 30/5 = 6.

9. The median is:
A. The most frequent value
B. The middle value when data is ordered
C. The average of all values
D. The difference between max and min
Answer: B. The middle value when data is ordered

10. What is the median of: 1, 3, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9?
A. 3
B. 6
C. 7
D. 5.5
Answer: B. 6
Explanation: Ordered: 1, 3, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9. Middle value (4th) = 6.

11. The mode is:
A. The middle value
B. The most frequently occurring value
C. The average value
D. The largest value
Answer: B. The most frequently occurring value

12. In a skewed distribution, which measure of central tendency is most affected by outliers?
A. Median
B. Mode
C. Mean
D. All equally
Answer: C. Mean
Explanation: Mean is sensitive to extreme values; median and mode are robust.

PART C — MEASURES OF DISPERSION

13. The range is:
A. The average value
B. The difference between maximum and minimum
C. The middle value
D. The most frequent value
Answer: B. The difference between maximum and minimum

13. The variance measures:
A. The average value
B. The spread of data around the mean
C. The middle value
D. The most frequent value
Answer: B. The spread of data around the mean

14. The standard deviation is:
A. The square root of variance
B. The square of variance
C. The same as variance
D. The mean of squared deviations
Answer: A. The square root of variance

15. If variance = 16, standard deviation = ?
A. 2
B. 4
C. 8
D. 256
Answer: B. 4
Explanation: SD = √variance = √16 = 4.

16. The interquartile range (IQR) is:
A. Q3 - Q1
B. Q1 - Q3
C. Q2 - Q1
D. Max - Min
Answer: A. Q3 - Q1

17. Which measure of spread is most resistant to outliers?
A. Range
B. Variance
C. Standard deviation
D. IQR
Answer: D. IQR
Explanation: IQR uses quartiles, not affected by extreme values.

PART D — DISTRIBUTIONS & INFERENCE

17. A normal distribution is:
A. Always skewed
B. Symmetric and bell-shaped
C. Always uniform
D. Always bimodal
Answer: B. Symmetric and bell-shaped

18. In a normal distribution, mean = median = mode.
A. True
B. False
Answer: A. True

19. The empirical rule states that for a normal distribution:
A. 68% within 1σ, 95% within 2σ, 99.7% within 3σ
B. 50% within 1σ, 75% within 2σ, 90% within 3σ
C. 50% within 1σ, 95% within 2σ, 99% within 3σ
D. 68% within 1σ, 90% within 2σ, 95% within 3σ
Answer: A. 68% within 1σ, 95% within 2σ, 99.7% within 3σ

19. A z-score represents:
A. The raw score
B. How many standard deviations a value is from the mean
C. The percentile rank
D. The probability of the value
Answer: B. How many standard deviations a value is from the mean

20. A 95% confidence interval means:
A. 95% of data falls in the interval
B. We are 95% confident the population parameter lies in the interval
C. 95% of samples will have the same mean
D. There is a 95% chance the sample mean equals the population mean
Answer: B. We are 95% confident the population parameter lies in the interval

21. The p-value is:
A. The probability the null hypothesis is true
B. The probability of observing data as extreme as (or more extreme than) observed, assuming H₀ is true
C. The probability the alternative hypothesis is true
D. The significance level
Answer: B. The probability of observing data as extreme as (or more extreme than) observed, assuming H₀ is true

22. If p-value < α (significance level), we:
A. Fail to reject H₀
B. Reject H₀
C. Accept H₀
D. Increase sample size
Answer: B. Reject H₀

23. Type I error is:
A. Rejecting a true null hypothesis
B. Failing to reject a false null hypothesis
C. Accepting a false null hypothesis
D. Accepting a true null hypothesis
Answer: A. Rejecting a true null hypothesis

24. Type II error is:
A. Rejecting a true null hypothesis
B. Failing to reject a false null hypothesis
C. Accepting a true null hypothesis
D. Rejecting a false null hypothesis
Answer: B. Failing to reject a false null hypothesis

25. The power of a test is:
A. P(Type I error)
B. P(Type II error)
C. 1 - P(Type II error)
D. 1 - P(Type I error)
Answer: C. 1 - P(Type II error)
Explanation: Power = probability of correctly rejecting a false null hypothesis.
