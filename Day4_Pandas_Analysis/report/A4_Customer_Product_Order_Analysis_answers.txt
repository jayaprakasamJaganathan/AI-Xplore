Part C — Combining Data

12. Merge orders with customers.
    → orders_customers = pd.merge(orders, customers, on='Customer_ID', how='left')

13. Merge the resulting DataFrame with products.
    → final_df = pd.merge(orders_customers, products, on='Product_ID', how='left')

14. Create a final consolidated DataFrame.
    → Contains: Order_ID, Customer_ID, Customer_Name, City, Age, Gender,
      Product_ID, Product_Name, Category, Unit_Price, Quantity, Order_Date,
      Gross_Amount, Net_Amount

    Bonus derived columns:
    → Gross_Amount = Quantity × Unit_Price
    → Discount_Amount = Gross_Amount × Discount / 100
    → Net_Amount = Gross_Amount - Discount_Amount

    Business insights:
    → Total revenue: final_df['Net_Amount'].sum()
    → Top customers by revenue
    → Top products by revenue
    → Revenue by city

Part D — Business Calculations

15. Create: Order_Value = Price × Quantity
    → final_df['Order_Value'] = final_df['Quantity'] * final_df['Unit_Price']

16. Calculate total revenue.
    → final_df['Order_Value'].sum()

17. Calculate average order value.
    → final_df['Order_Value'].mean()

18. Find total revenue by product category.
    → final_df.groupby('Category')['Order_Value'].sum().sort_values(ascending=False)

19. Find total revenue by city.
    → final_df.groupby('City')['Order_Value'].sum().sort_values(ascending=False)

20. Find the top 10 customers by spending.
    → final_df.groupby('Customer_ID')['Order_Value'].sum().nlargest(10)

21. Find the top 10 products by revenue.
    → final_df.groupby('Product_Name')['Order_Value'].sum().nlargest(10)

22. Find the most frequently purchased product.
    → final_df['Product_Name'].value_counts().idxmax()

23. Find the city with the highest number of customers.
    → final_df['City'].value_counts().idxmax()

24. Find the city generating the highest revenue.
    → final_df.groupby('City')['Order_Value'].sum().idxmax()

Part E — Customer Analysis

25. Divide customers into age groups.
    18–30, 31–45, 46–60, 61+
    → def age_group(age): ...
    → final_df['Age_Group'] = final_df['Age'].apply(age_group)

26. Calculate revenue generated by each age group.
    → final_df.groupby('Age_Group')['Order_Value'].sum()

27. Compare spending between male and female customers.
    → final_df.groupby('Gender')['Order_Value'].mean()

28. Identify high-value customers.
    → high_value = final_df.groupby('Customer_ID')['Order_Value'].sum()
    → high_value[high_value > high_value.quantile(0.9)]

29. Determine the percentage of customers who have placed more than one order.
    → order_counts = final_df.groupby('Customer_ID')['Order_ID'].nunique()
    → (order_counts > 1).mean() * 100

Part F — Advanced Analysis

30. Create a customer spending ranking.
    → final_df.groupby('Customer_ID')['Order_Value'].sum().rank(ascending=False)

31. Identify the top 10% of customers based on total spending.
    → spending = final_df.groupby('Customer_ID')['Order_Value'].sum()
    → spending[spending >= spending.quantile(0.9)]

32. Find the most profitable product category for each city.
    → final_df.groupby(['City', 'Category'])['Order_Value'].sum().groupby('City').idxmax()

33. Find the most popular product in each category.
    → final_df.groupby(['Category', 'Product_Name'])['Order_ID'].count().groupby('Category').idxmax()

34. Calculate monthly revenue.
    → final_df['Month'] = final_df['Order_Date'].dt.to_period('M')
    → final_df.groupby('Month')['Order_Value'].sum()

35. Identify the month with the highest revenue.
    → final_df.groupby('Month')['Order_Value'].sum().idxmax()
